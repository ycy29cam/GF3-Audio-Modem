In halls where Newton’s whispers dwell,
The stones of time and logic swell.
Within the courts, by River Cam,
A forge of minds, precise and calm.

Steel and silicon, thought entwined,
Ideas shaped in circuits lined.
In Dyson’s den or Hopkin’s frame,
They chase not gold, but deeper flame.

From turbines turning Fenland breeze,
To quantum shifts in energies,
The whispering labs, the humming bays,
All pulse with knowledge's steady blaze.

Where bridge meets byte and code meets clay,
They craft the world in bold array.
Not theory locked in towers alone,
But function built in glass and stone.

The undergrads with sleepless eyes,
Who wrestle doubt where answers lie—
They learn not just to build and break,
But ask what good their hands will make.

In lectures crisp and corners dim,
The past walks with the future’s whim.
Here Turing’s ghost, in circuits terse,
Still weaves through logic's silent verse.

And far beyond the city's breath,
Their work defies decay and death.
From power grids to Mars’s skies,
They stretch the bounds where wonder lies.

So here's to those in chalk and steel,
Who probe what’s true, and shape what’s real.
In Cambridge walls of weathered grey,
They build the tools for light and day.
